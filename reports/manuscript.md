# Benchmark choice and quotation persistence in county maize–urea price monitoring: Evidence from Liaoning, China

Authors and affiliations: [To be completed by the authors.]

## Abstract

Benchmark choice changes agricultural price-monitoring signals, and persistent quotations affect both the historical threshold and later classifications. We analyze Liaoning maize–urea quotations over 2018–2026 using five benchmarks on the same 204 complete monitoring dates. Aggregate attainment for all twelve counties ranges from 34.31% to 99.02%, and aggregate alerts for the six screened counties range from zero to 131 dates. These differences persist under a common 50% county-share threshold and all twelve single-county deletions. The quotation screen flags six of twelve counties. Jianping records 109 identical urea quotations over 1,096 days; Jianping and Qingyuan account for 54.64% of accumulated shortfalls under the 2018–2020 benchmark. Four representative unchanged-urea runs overlap that historical window, linking quotation persistence to benchmark construction. All five paired excess-incidence intervals are positive for the original twelve counties. After excluding Jianping and Qingyuan, the 2020 interval is positive and the other four include zero; after the full screen, all five include zero. Historically flagged counties update less frequently in both later periods, while every long unchanged run coincides with updating same-product peers. The results show that benchmark selection and quotation history determine the monitoring signal. Reports should state the historical window, county membership and alert threshold alongside the quotation histories.

## 1 Introduction

Agricultural price monitors turn reported quotations into signals that guide investigation of local price conditions. Two choices shape those signals: the historical prices used as a benchmark and the updating histories of the component quotations. Repeated values enter both the historical threshold and the later price index. We quantify how historical-window choice and county membership change the classifications and alert dates generated from the same price records.

An output–input price ratio relates the quoted value of a crop to an input cost. The World Bank's fertilizer-affordability measure compares fertilizer and food prices with a historical average (World Bank, 2024). We follow maize purchase and urea quotations jointly at county level and compare each county with its own historical exchange terms. Our benchmark defines a classification threshold, whereas an index reference period used solely to rescale a series leaves relative movements unchanged (ILO et al., 2020). FAO's price-anomaly indicator instead compares quarterly and annual growth with month-specific historical distributions (Baquedano, 2015). We test historical-window choice and month-specific comparison separately to identify changes in the reference level and seasonal alignment.

The observation unit is a monitoring quotation. The meaning of a repeated value depends on its observation basis: a current local price, an earlier reported quotation, or a sampling point awaiting new price information. Measurement studies show that the observation process affects the apparent frequency of price changes (Eichenbaum et al., 2014). The Consumer Price Index Manual treats carrying a previous price forward as a procedure for handling missing information (ILO et al., 2020). We use product-specific updating, equal values across source changes and national-turn timing to identify persistent reporting patterns in the county archive.

China's grain-price monitoring rules specify observations on the fifth, fifteenth and twenty-fifth days of each month and assign review and aggregation responsibilities (National Development and Reform Commission, 2011). Agricultural-input rules distinguish sampling-day transactions, posted prices for available stock and the latest transaction when an item is temporarily unavailable (Price Monitoring Center, 2015). Liaoning's monitoring-point review introduced trial reporting, site visits and replacements in response to collection difficulties (Liaoning Provincial Development and Reform Commission, 2022). These procedures make the sampling enterprise and the date of its underlying price information relevant to repeated quotations.

The reference windows cover three complete archive years after the 2016 maize procurement reform and before the 2021 fertilizer-price surge. We use the full 2018–2020 window, remove each endpoint year in turn, compare with the closest complete year, and match observations by calendar month. This design separates changes in the historical level from within-year variation. For agricultural information services, the usefulness of information and the design of delivery are central concerns (Shepherd, 1997; Galtier et al., 2014). Alert dates and episodes translate the benchmark differences into the signals a monitoring service would review.

We show that benchmark selection changes attainment and alert frequency even after every single-county deletion. The quotation screen identifies persistent county–product records that enter the historical reference as well as the later index. Historically flagged counties continue to update less frequently in later periods, and same-product quotations change elsewhere in the province during every long unchanged run. These findings connect benchmark construction to the reporting histories behind the monitoring signal.

## 2 Institutional background and economic framework

China replaced temporary maize procurement in the northeastern provinces and Inner Mongolia with market-oriented purchasing and subsidies in 2016 (National Development and Reform Commission, 2017). The 2018–2020 window supplies three complete archive years after that reform and before the 2021 fertilizer-price surge. In 2021, domestic fertilizer-stabilization measures addressed production inputs, reserves, transport and supervision, while mandatory export inspection for 29 ten-digit fertilizer customs codes took effect on 15 October (National Development and Reform Commission and other departments, 2021; General Administration of Customs, 2021). These dated changes define the historical windows and later price environment relevant to the study.

Fertilizer shocks provide the economic setting. Hu et al. (2025) separate energy-related and urea-specific components of Chinese price movements; Willwerth et al. (2025) find heterogeneous Kenyan agro-dealer responses to import-price increases and decreases. We track the input quotation together with maize to determine which recorded component changes accompany improvement in local exchange terms.

Let \(P_{C,it}\) and \(P_{U,it}\) denote the maize purchase and domestic-urea quotations in the same currency per kilogram. Their exchange ratio is

$$
Q_{it}=\frac{P_{C,it}}{P_{U,it}}. \tag{1}
$$

The ratio represents kilograms of urea at the quoted monetary value of one kilogram of maize. For fixed fertilizer quantity \(u\) and maize output sold \(Y\), fertilizer expense divided by maize revenue is \((u/Y)/Q\). Fertilizer profitability combines yield response with output and input prices (Ragasa et al., 2025); Northeast Chinese spring-maize recommendations use soil-fertility groups and response functions (Bao et al., 2020). The exchange ratio measures the quoted relative-price component of this relationship.

During a constant-urea segment, \(\Delta\log Q_{it}=\Delta\log P_{C,it}\). The maize quotation supplies all recorded numerical movement in the ratio within that segment, while the unchanged urea level continues to determine its level. When that value also occupies part of the historical window, quotation persistence influences both the benchmark and the later comparison. Examining the two product histories therefore explains which observed adjustments underlie an index change.

## 3 Data and measurement

### 3.1 Archive, calendars and county groups

The Liaoning archive records mixed-grade maize purchase and domestic-urea quotations from 5 January 2018 to 25 September 2026 (Liaoning Provincial Development and Reform Commission, n.d.). The normalized data contain 9,450 records over 315 monitoring dates: 8,987 positive values, 453 original zeros and ten missing values. Fifteen geographic slots appear per date, with sixteen identifiers across the full record. Product definitions, county identifiers, original numerical strings, observation states and source labels are recorded. We calculate logarithms from positive price pairs.

The original twelve counties are those shared by the historical comparison endpoints. Their 2018–2020 calendar has 108 complete dates. The later calendar has 207 scheduled observations, of which 204 are complete. We also report eleven counties excluding Jianping as a sensitivity group. Removing Jianping and Qingyuan through the low-update branch leaves ten counties. The combined screen also removes Sujiatun, Dashiqiao, Zhangwu and Dawa, leaving Faku, Zhuanghe, Fengcheng, Dengta, Changtu and Kaiyuan. This six-county group provides the comparison after the complete screen. We recalculate each group’s aggregate, attainment dates and county residuals on the original completeness mask, so membership comparisons use the same observation calendar.

Individual shortfall sums use all valid county pairs, including observed county cells when the aggregate is unavailable. Update-rate denominators require a positive scheduled predecessor and source continuity. Constant-price histories use each series' positive observations, with gaps interrupting a sequence.

### 3.2 Original values and external comparison

County quotations are expressed in CNY per 500 g. Multiplication by two converts them to CNY per kilogram and preserves their ratio. The domestic-urea field identifies the product and unit; its transaction-stage designation is unspecified. We identify equal quotations and updates from their original decimal values. Screening runs stop at the source change between 25 January and 5 February 2024. Descriptive numerical-persistence segments continue across that boundary when consecutive scheduled positive values remain equal. Zeros and missing planned observations end a continuous observed segment; equal values separated by a gap receive a separate observed-span record.

National Bureau of Statistics circulation-survey reports supply second-grade yellow-maize and small-/medium-small-particle urea quotations in CNY per tonne (National Bureau of Statistics of China, 2024a, 2024b). Their wholesale scope and specifications differ from the county fields. We compare proportional changes within each series, which places their movements on the same dimensionless scale. Endpoints are matched to nominal dekad dates, the fifth, fifteenth and twenty-fifth; unavailable national endpoints remain missing. Matching national endpoints to the middle of each reporting interval leaves all screening flags unchanged.

We use the national series to identify wider market movement during an unchanged county quotation run. The screen measures proportional national price movement during each unchanged county quotation run. With partial national coverage, the observed maximum-to-minimum range is a lower bound on the full national range over that window. An observed national amplitude above 20% records substantial movement in the matched national series while the county quotation stays fixed.

The source archive contains 610 responses, including six original HTTP failures. Nineteen selected original-page checks link normalized records back to the reported cells. Additional commodities are grouped by source-local identity, exact specification and unit, with capture coverage recorded for each group. The complete observation records and date-level results accompany the analysis as replication materials.

### 3.3 Benchmarks and measurement

The 2018–2020 benchmark uses each county's mean log exchange ratio over all three complete reference years. It averages historical exchange terms across three annual cycles after the maize procurement reform and before the 2021 fertilizer-price surge.

The two-year windows remove the endpoints symmetrically. The 2019–2020 benchmark excludes 2018 and tests the influence of the earliest reference year; the 2018–2019 benchmark excludes 2020 and tests the influence of the latest. The 2020 benchmark supplies the closest complete annual comparison before the 2021 fertilizer shock. This timing rationale matters because Liaoning's autumn-grain survey reports a maize purchase price of 1.18 CNY per 500 g on 25 November 2020, compared with 0.90 a year earlier (Liaoning Provincial Development and Reform Commission, 2020).

The month-specific 2018–2020 benchmark compares each later observation with the same calendar month in the three reference years. We quantify the contribution of month indicators to separate within-year variation from differences in historical levels. Table 1 gives abbreviated benchmark and group labels for the tabulated results.

For county benchmark \(b_i\), define

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{it}&=\log Q_{it}-b_i,\qquad I_{it}=\exp(g_{it}),\\
G_t&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_{it},\qquad A_t=\exp(G_t),\\
H_t&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\{g_{it}<0\}.
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
$$

For the month-specific benchmark, \(b_i\) takes the target month's value. Aggregate attainment means \(G_t\geq0\). Mean \(H_t\) on attainment dates measures the share of counties below their own benchmarks while their aggregate attains its benchmark. County shortfalls sum \(\max(-g_{it},0)\) across valid observations and have units of log-point monitoring periods. Attainment on an individual date defines the aggregate condition used here; the alert rule in Section 4.5 adds the requirement of three consecutive qualifying observations.

## 4 Empirical strategy

### 4.1 Historical-residual reference along the observed aggregate path

We hold the observed later aggregate path constant and replace county deviations with historical residual vectors. On donor date \(s\),

$$
r_{is}=g_{is}-G_s,\qquad
g^{\mathrm{ref}}_{it}=G_t+r_{i,s(t)}. \tag{3}
$$

We sample whole county-date vectors in non-circular moving blocks, keeping cross-county co-movement and short-run dependence together (Künsch, 1989). Six monitoring periods form the main block length. Each configuration uses 1,999 draws on the scheduled calendar, followed by the common completeness mask. Observed and reference statistics use the same attainment dates. We compare observed below-benchmark incidence with the distribution generated by historical county deviations along the observed aggregate path.

We report the mean reference incidence and its two-sided 90% interval, from the fifth to the ninety-fifth percentile. The one-sided upper-tail estimate is \(\widehat p=(K+1)/(R+1)\), where \(K\) counts reference statistics at least as large as the observation and \(R\) counts valid draws. Ties are included. The fixed reference varies county deviations at the estimated historical threshold; the paired comparison below also varies the historical benchmark. Appendix A.1 gives the calendar alignment used in the annual and month-specific comparisons.

### 4.2 Paired benchmark re-estimation

We resample historical county-date vectors and re-estimate each county's benchmark. Each draw recalculates the later aggregate path and attainment dates. Historical residuals are recomputed relative to the re-estimated benchmarks and centered across counties on every donor date. For each benchmark draw, we sample one independent historical residual path and compare both incidence statistics on the recalculated attainment dates.

Let \(O^*\) and \(K_{\mathrm{ref}}^*\) denote the observed and reference below-benchmark county–date counts, and \(T^*\) the attainment-date count. The paired difference is

$$
D^*=\overline H^{\,\mathrm{obs},*}-\overline H^{\,\mathrm{ref},*}
=\frac{O^*-K_{\mathrm{ref}}^*}{NT^*}. \tag{4}
$$

We report the mean difference, a two-sided 95% percentile interval and the share of draws with \(D^*>0\). Each paired draw combines re-estimated historical means with a sampled historical residual path. The mean and interval therefore summarize both sources of variation. We identify excess incidence with a paired interval above zero and classify intervals containing zero separately.

The fixed reference isolates county-level residual variation; the paired comparison adds benchmark estimation. For the ten-county group under the 2019–2020 benchmark, the fixed-reference tail estimate is 0.010. Benchmark re-estimation produces a paired interval containing zero, showing that historical-mean variation changes the classification of excess incidence. Table 3 compares the three main county groups on identical dates. Appendix A.1 gives the resampling conventions; complete block-length results accompany the replication materials.

### 4.3 Resampling precision

We use 1,999 draws for every comparison in Table 3 and extend six selected comparisons to 9,999 draws: the 2018–2020 benchmark for twelve, eleven and six counties; the 2020 benchmark for ten and six counties; and the month-specific benchmark for eleven counties. Section 5.2 reports the extended comparisons. Appendix A.2 defines tail estimates, Monte Carlo standard errors and percentile conventions.

### 4.4 Quotation screening and episode updates

The low-update branch flags counties with either product's update rate strictly below one-third of that product's median across the original twelve counties. Rates divide changes in recorded decimal values by positive, scheduled, same-source adjacent comparisons. The median remains tied to the original twelve-county group as counties are removed. The second branch flags source-specific constant runs longer than 36 observations with matched national price amplitude strictly above 20%. Amplitude is \(\max(P)/\min(P)-1\), evaluated from the original decimal prices.

With partial national coverage, an observed range above the amplitude threshold confirms the run-amplitude branch; a lower observed range with unobserved periods leaves it unresolved. The two branches combine through a logical OR, so a low-update flag confirms the classification independently of national coverage. The threshold grid combines update-rate fractions of one-quarter, one-third, one-half and two-thirds; minimum run lengths strictly above 24, 36 and 48 observations; and national-amplitude thresholds strictly above 10%, 20% and 30%. These choices give 36 settings. We apply each setting to all twelve counties and recalculate the comparisons for each resulting county group.

Below-benchmark spells under the 2018–2020 benchmark end at a changed classification, invalid price pair or source boundary. A spell of length \(\ell\) has \(\ell-1\) internal comparison opportunities. Updates compare quotations within the spell and exclude the entering transition. We group spells by length and calculate internal update rates and zero-update shares for spells containing at least two observations. County rates pool changes and eligible adjacent comparisons.

### 4.5 Monitoring consequences

Rule i triggers from the third consecutive complete scheduled date with \(G_t<0\), then continues while that condition holds. Alert dates begin with the third qualifying observation. Rule ii triggers on \(3k_t\geq N\), where \(k_t\) counts below-benchmark counties. Its minimum counts are four for the original twelve-county group, four for the ten-county group and two for the six counties passing the combined screen. A common 50% sensitivity uses \(2k_t\geq N\), requiring six, five and three counties, respectively.

Missing scheduled observations reset the aggregate streak and end an active episode. Any false or missing date ends a trigger episode; episodes continue across a source boundary while the trigger condition holds. Trigger fractions use 204 complete dates, and the date-level records preserve all 207 slots.

### 4.6 Monthly variation and quotation timing

We measure monthly variation in the complete 2018–2020 panel, which has 108 historical dates and 1,296 county–date pairs for all twelve counties. The six screened counties supply 648 pairs. The primary outcome is \(\log Q\), with equal weight for each valid county–date pair. We estimate a month-indicator model, a county-and-year model, and a county-year-month model. Let \(\mathrm{SSE}_M\), \(\mathrm{SSE}_R\) and \(\mathrm{SSE}_F\) denote their residual sums of squares, respectively, and let \(\mathrm{TSS}\) be the sum of squares centered on the overall outcome mean. The raw month share is \(1-\mathrm{SSE}_M/\mathrm{TSS}\); the incremental share is \((\mathrm{SSE}_R-\mathrm{SSE}_F)/\mathrm{TSS}\); the partial share divides the same reduction by \(\mathrm{SSE}_R\). Level ratios, equal-date weights and the six-county panel provide measurement checks.

For quotation timing, we average observed national urea prices by month without interpolation. The primary rule requires at least two observed dekads per month, an opposite preceding movement, two consecutive movements in the new direction, and a cumulative change of at least 5% from the extremum. Flat months, insufficient monthly coverage and calendar gaps interrupt the direction sequence. A national turn becomes available on the latest publication date of the observations needed to confirm it. Each county update is matched to the most recent same-direction turn available by its monitoring date. Three- and six-month distances are measured from the extremum month. A 10% threshold and complete three-dekad coverage provide checks. The rules apply to all twelve counties; Appendix A.3 lists all six Jianping urea changes.

### 4.7 County influence and comparisons across time and place

We delete each of the twelve counties once under all five benchmarks, giving sixty comparisons in addition to the original twelve-county controls. Every deletion uses the original 204 complete dates, six-period blocks and the same 1,999 sampling paths. County means, residuals and aggregate classifications are recalculated for the eleven remaining counties. This design reports the influence of every county under every benchmark.

We then apply the quotation screen to county observations from 5 January 2018 to 25 December 2020, matching national information published by that endpoint. The update-rate cutoff is one-third of each product's historical twelve-county median; the run branch uses more than 36 observations and national amplitude above 20%. County membership is fixed before comparing 2021–2023 and January 2024–September 2026. Update rates pool changes and eligible same-source adjacent transitions within each period. Later alerts use the 2018–2020 county benchmarks and the original complete-date mask.

Finally, every same-source unchanged run longer than 36 observations is compared with the same product, specification and unit in the other eleven original counties. The target county is excluded from this fixed comparison group. We calculate its median on dates with positive quotations from all eleven peers. We report median amplitude, complete-date coverage and the number of peers with at least one eligible update during each run.

## 5 Results

### 5.1 The complete screen flags six counties

The low-update branch identifies Jianping for maize and urea, and Qingyuan for urea. Jianping changes its maize quotation in 35 of 311 valid comparisons and its urea quotation in six of 311; Qingyuan changes maize in 48 of 313 (15.34%) and urea in eight of 313 (2.56%). Both Jianping products fall below their product-specific low-update cutoffs, whereas Qingyuan enters the branch through urea. The county-series medians are 37.40% for maize and 11.18% for urea, giving one-third thresholds of 12.47% and 3.73%. These product-specific rates define membership of the ten-county group.

### 5.3 Persistent quotation levels determine which product moves the ratio

Under the 2018–2020 benchmark, accumulated shortfall is 47.54 log-point monitoring periods. Jianping contributes 22.71 and Qingyuan 3.27, together accounting for 54.64% of the unrounded total. The other ten counties contribute 21.56. These sums use valid county observations, including dates without a complete aggregate.

Jianping reports 1.00 CNY per 500 g for urea on 109 consecutive observations from 25 April 2018 to 25 April 2021, spanning 1,096 elapsed calendar days. The 2018–2020 benchmark also includes eleven earlier quotations of 0.75, so it combines two recorded urea levels. Urea rises to 1.10 on 5 May 2021 and to 1.40 on 15 August 2021. The domestic specification and quotation unit remain the same across the latter change.

A later urea level of 1.50 persists for 94 observations from 15 April 2022 to 15 November 2024, crossing the archive source boundary. ## 7 Discussion

### 7.1 Benchmarks determine the historical question and the alerts

Benchmark selection and quotation history jointly determine the monitoring signal. Historical-window choice changes attainment and alert dates, while county screening changes the excess-incidence classification. The historical windows contain different relative-price conditions. The 2018–2019 comparison classifies almost every complete later date for all twelve counties as attained, whereas the 2020 comparison classifies roughly one-third. Benchmark choice therefore determines which dates enter county-incidence summaries and which trigger an alert.

The two references answer different questions. The fixed reference varies historical county deviations along the observed aggregate trajectory. The paired experiment also varies benchmark means and attainment dates. For the ten-county group, the 2019–2020 fixed-reference tail estimate is 0.010. Adding historical-mean variation produces a paired interval containing zero and changes the excess-incidence classification.

All twelve counties have strictly positive paired intervals under all five benchmarks. Removing Jianping and Qingyuan produces a positive interval for the one-year 2020 benchmark and intervals containing zero for the other four. Removing all six flagged counties produces intervals containing zero under every benchmark in the 1,999-draw comparison. The selected 9,999-draw checks for 2018–2020 and 2020 give the same classification. County membership therefore determines which benchmark comparisons show excess incidence.

### 7.2 Quotation persistence links historical levels to monitoring signals

The observation basis links a repeated quotation to its market meaning: a current price, an earlier quotation, or a sampling point awaiting new information. The agricultural-input rules explicitly distinguish a current transaction, an available-stock posted price and the most recent transaction for an unavailable item (Price Monitoring Center, 2015). The CPI Manual requires complete re-recording even when a price is unchanged and describes the effect of carrying old observations forward (ILO et al., 2020, paragraphs 5.65 and 6.61).

The county patterns identify different priorities for checking that record. Jianping has low numerical updating in both products, directing attention to the county's reporting and sampling-point history. Qingyuan's maize rate exceeds the maize low-update cutoff, while its urea rate is below the urea cutoff, directing attention to the product record. Jianping's unchanged 1.50-CNY urea value crosses the January–February 2024 source-system change with the same specification and unit. Its later gap separates the 94-observation segment from another 44 equal observations. National-turn matching records dispersed update dates and changes with the monthly coverage rule. The verification unit is therefore a county–product observation with its sampling enterprise, price basis and transaction date.

Persistent quotations also enter the benchmark itself. Four of the six representative runs overlap 2018–2020, and Jianping's three-year reference combines long periods at two urea levels. During a later unchanged-urea segment, all recorded exchange-ratio movement comes from maize, while the earlier urea quotations determine the historical comparison level. The reporting history therefore affects both threshold construction and later classifications. Liaoning's documented trial reporting, site visits and monitoring-point replacements provide an administrative route for checking the flagged histories (Liaoning Provincial Development and Reform Commission, 2022).

### 7.3 Thresholds change monitoring workload

A one-third share rule requires four of ten counties, or 40%; three of ten represents 30%. A common 50% threshold aligns the minimum triggering share across the three groups, and substantial benchmark differences remain. For the six screened counties, the 2020 window generates aggregate alerts on 131 dates, and the 2018–2019 window generates zero. The historical window therefore determines the volume of alerts generated for review.

Fewer alert dates can accompany more episodes when a higher threshold splits a long trigger run. This occurs for the twelve-county sample under the 2019–2020 benchmark and the six-county sample under the month-specific benchmark. Reporting dates and episodes together shows both the duration and recurrence of a monitoring signal.

Changing county membership changes both the denominator and the aggregate path. Under the 2018–2020 benchmark, removing the two low-update counties reduces aggregate alerts from nineteen to twelve monitoring dates; under the 2020 benchmark, applying the full screen increases them from 126 to 131. Monitors should display the county group alongside each alert series.

### 7.4 Contribution to agricultural price information

The World Bank’s affordability indicator compares an input–output price relationship with a stated historical average (World Bank, 2024); our county results show how alternative historical averages change local incidence. Baquedano’s (2015) anomaly indicator assesses seasonal and annual price growth; our alert comparison holds observations fixed and varies the historical threshold and sample membership. Eichenbaum et al. (2014) examine apparent small changes associated with measurement error; our long unchanged sequences identify observation records for follow-up. Galtier et al. (2014) discuss the design and impacts of agricultural market information systems; our date and episode counts show a consequence of design choices for potential monitoring workload.

## 8 Measurement basis and screening interpretation

We measure the quoted exchange terms between maize and urea in the twelve-county archive. At fixed fertilizer quantity and maize sales, a lower exchange ratio raises the fertilizer-expense share of maize revenue. The screen identifies county–product records for checking against sampling-enterprise, transaction-date and observation-status information. The inventory covers 31 exact product/specification/unit groups, with capture coverage recorded for each group and matched national comparisons for maize purchase and domestic urea. We compare proportional movement within each series to accommodate differences in product specifications and survey scope. Under partial coverage, the observed national amplitude provides a lower bound on the amplitude over the full matched window.

We developed the quotation screen after exploratory inspection of the data and applied the same rules to all twelve counties. The 36-setting grid quantifies the effect of threshold choice: Zhangwu is flagged at 10% and 20% amplitudes and remains unresolved at 30%. The 2020 benchmark uses 36 monitoring dates from the year immediately preceding the fertilizer-price surge. Its positive ten-county paired interval identifies excess incidence relative to that year’s exchange terms. Within-year resampling diagnostics and extended draws document the numerical precision of this comparison.

## 9 Conclusions

The results show that historical benchmark choice determines the monitoring classifications and alert frequency produced from the same county quotations. Across five benchmarks, attainment for all twelve counties ranges from 34.31% to 99.02%, while aggregate alerts for the six screened counties range from zero to 131 dates. The differences persist under a common 50% county-share threshold and all twelve symmetric county deletions. Choosing the historical window sets both the comparison level and the workload generated by monitoring alerts.

Persistent quotations connect historical benchmark levels to later county signals. The combined screen flags six of twelve counties. Jianping records 109 identical urea quotations over 1,096 days, and Jianping and Qingyuan contribute 54.64% of accumulated shortfalls under the 2018–2020 benchmark. Four representative runs overlap that window, linking quotation histories to the benchmark itself. Counties flagged from historical observations continue to update less frequently in both later periods; every long unchanged run coincides with an updating same-product peer elsewhere in the province. These findings identify county–product records for checking the sampling point and the date and basis of the reported quotation.

County membership changes the classification of excess incidence. All five paired intervals are positive for the original twelve counties. Excluding Jianping and Qingyuan produces a positive interval under the one-year 2020 benchmark and intervals containing zero under the other four; the full screen produces intervals containing zero under every benchmark. These results identify quotation history and county composition as determinants of the distributional signal. Monitoring reports should show aggregate attainment beside county incidence and report alert dates together with episodes. Stating the reference window, county membership, update history and trigger threshold makes the resulting signals comparable and identifies the quotation records to check first.

## Appendix A Supporting definitions and quotation changes

### A.1 Calendar alignment and paired resampling

The fixed-reference paths cover all 207 planned later observations before the original 204-complete-date mask is applied. The historical residual vector includes every county in the comparison group. Annual benchmarks use non-circular blocks within their own historical windows. For the month-specific benchmark, each block matches both its starting month and its fifth/fifteenth/twenty-fifth reporting position, then follows the historical seasonal sequence. The seasonally aligned outer blocks fill the historical target calendar, and the inner residual paths use the same alignment on the later calendar. County groups share date indices within a benchmark and block length and recalculate their own aggregate path, attainment set and denominators.

In a paired draw, the resampled history first supplies new county means. Historical residual candidates are then recomputed and centered across counties on each donor date. One independent residual path is sampled for that draw, and both incidence statistics are evaluated on the same recalculated attainment set. We calculate incidence statistics for draws with at least one attainment date. The mean difference, its interval and the positive-draw fraction summarize the joint distribution of historical-mean variation and residual-path variation. The positive fraction reports the share of paired differences above zero; the fixed-reference tail estimate counts reference statistics at least as large as the observation.

### A.2 Tail estimates and numerical precision

The fixed-reference estimate is \(\widehat p=(K+1)/(R+1)\), including ties. Its displayed resolution with 1,999 valid draws is 0.0005. At a zero tail count we display ≤0.0005 †; at a complete tail count we display ≥0.9995 †. The underlying plus-one values at those two boundaries are 0.0005 and 1.0000. A one-sided 95% exact binomial bound is about 0.00150 for a zero tail and about 0.99850 for a complete tail. These bounds describe numerical tail-probability precision.

For interior counts, the Monte Carlo standard error uses the sampled tail fraction \(K/R\). Percentile intervals use linear interpolation. The fixed-reference interval is two-sided 90%, and the paired interval is two-sided 95%. A rounded zero endpoint is evaluated using the full-precision result; exact zero belongs to the equality boundary. The selected 9,999-draw comparisons are reported separately from the uniformly 1,999-draw main table.
